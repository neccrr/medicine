# Pharmacology — High-Yield Summary

## Pharmacokinetics Basics
- **ADME**: Absorption → Distribution → Metabolism → Excretion. Bioavailability is the fraction of an oral dose that reaches systemic circulation unchanged.
- **First-pass metabolism**: oral drugs are metabolized by intestinal/hepatic enzymes (often CYP3A4) before reaching systemic circulation — reduces bioavailability.
- **CYP450 interactions**: inhibitors (e.g., grapefruit juice, macrolides, azoles) raise levels of co-administered CYP substrates; inducers (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin) lower them.
- **Half-life**: time for plasma concentration to fall by 50%; ~4–5 half-lives to reach steady state or fully eliminate a drug.

## Cardiovascular Drugs
- **ACE inhibitors**: block conversion of angiotensin I → II, reducing vasoconstriction/aldosterone. Bradykinin accumulation causes dry cough and angioedema.
- **Beta-blockers**: block beta-1 receptors, reducing heart rate, contractility, and myocardial oxygen demand — mortality-reducing in HFrEF and post-MI.
- **Statins**: inhibit HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting step in cholesterol synthesis; watch for myopathy/rhabdomyolysis, especially with CYP3A4 inhibitors.
- **Warfarin**: inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase, blocking synthesis of factors II, VII, IX, X and proteins C/S. Monitor with PT/INR; reverse urgently with 4-factor PCC + vitamin K.

## Antimicrobials & Toxicology
- **Beta-lactams**: inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding penicillin-binding proteins.
- **Vancomycin**: rapid infusion causes non-IgE histamine release ("red man syndrome") — slow the infusion, don't stop therapy.
- **Chloramphenicol**: causes "gray baby syndrome" in neonates due to immature hepatic glucuronidation and drug accumulation.
- **Antidotes to know**: acetaminophen → N-acetylcysteine; benzodiazepines → flumazenil (caution: seizure risk); opioids → naloxone; iron → deferoxamine; warfarin → 4-factor PCC + vitamin K.

## Endocrine Drugs
- **Sulfonylureas**: stimulate pancreatic insulin release independent of glucose — highest hypoglycemia risk among oral diabetes agents.
- **Metformin**: decreases hepatic gluconeogenesis and increases insulin sensitivity; low intrinsic hypoglycemia risk; avoid in significant renal impairment (lactic acidosis risk).
- **PPIs (e.g., omeprazole)**: irreversibly inhibit the gastric H+/K+ ATPase, suppressing acid secretion.

## Emergency Pharmacology
- **Anaphylaxis**: IM epinephrine (anterolateral thigh) is first-line — antihistamines and steroids are adjuncts, not substitutes.
- **Opioid overdose**: naloxone, an opioid receptor antagonist; may need repeat dosing given naloxone's shorter half-life relative to many opioids.
- **Benzodiazepine overdose**: flumazenil reverses sedation but can precipitate seizures in chronic users — reserve for select cases.
