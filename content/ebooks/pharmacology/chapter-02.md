# Chapter 2: Antimicrobial Essentials

Antimicrobial questions are usually testing one of three things: the
mechanism of action, a signature adverse effect, or which bug a drug
does *not* cover. Organize your review around those three angles rather
than memorizing drug names in isolation.

## Mechanism buckets

- **Cell wall synthesis inhibitors**: beta-lactams (penicillins,
  cephalosporins, carbapenems, monobactams) bind penicillin-binding
  proteins; vancomycin binds the D-Ala-D-Ala terminus of peptidoglycan
  precursors.
- **Protein synthesis inhibitors**: aminoglycosides and tetracyclines hit
  the 30S ribosomal subunit; macrolides, clindamycin, and chloramphenicol
  hit the 50S subunit.
- **Nucleic acid synthesis inhibitors**: fluoroquinolones inhibit DNA
  gyrase/topoisomerase IV; rifampin inhibits RNA polymerase;
  sulfonamides and trimethoprim block sequential steps of folate
  synthesis.

## Signature adverse effects worth memorizing

| Drug | Classic adverse effect |
|---|---|
| Vancomycin (rapid infusion) | Red man syndrome (histamine release) |
| Aminoglycosides | Nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity |
| Fluoroquinolones | Tendon rupture, QT prolongation |
| Tetracyclines | Tooth discoloration in children, photosensitivity |
| Chloramphenicol | Gray baby syndrome (neonates), aplastic anemia |
| Isoniazid | Peripheral neuropathy (give with B6/pyridoxine) |

## A fast way to think about coverage

Ask two questions: gram-positive or gram-negative, and does the
clinical picture suggest atypical organisms (walking pneumonia,
intracellular pathogens)? Beta-lactams generally cover typical
gram-positive/negative organisms but not atypicals; macrolides and
tetracyclines fill that atypical gap.

## Key teaching point

When a vignette describes a drug-specific toxicity (ototoxicity, gray
skin, tendon pain, photosensitivity), that toxicity is almost always the
actual question being asked — work backward from the adverse effect to
the drug class rather than trying to recall coverage spectrums from
scratch under time pressure.
