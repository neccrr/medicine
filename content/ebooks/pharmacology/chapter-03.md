# Chapter 3: Toxicology & Antidotes

Toxicology questions almost always follow the same template: a toxidrome
or lab finding, then "what's the antidote?" Build a mental table like the
one below and the answer becomes pattern recognition instead of recall.

## High-yield toxin → antidote pairs

| Toxin | Antidote | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Acetaminophen | N-acetylcysteine | Replenishes glutathione stores |
| Benzodiazepines | Flumazenil | Competitive GABA-A receptor antagonist |
| Opioids | Naloxone | Competitive opioid receptor antagonist |
| Iron | Deferoxamine | Chelates free iron |
| Warfarin | 4-factor PCC + vitamin K | Rapid clotting factor replacement |
| Heparin | Protamine sulfate | Binds and neutralizes heparin |
| Digoxin | Digoxin-specific antibody fragments | Binds free digoxin, inactivating it |
| Methanol / ethylene glycol | Fomepizole | Inhibits alcohol dehydrogenase |
| Organophosphates | Atropine + pralidoxime | Atropine blocks muscarinic effects; pralidoxime reactivates acetylcholinesterase |
| Cyanide | Hydroxocobalamin (or nitrites + thiosulfate) | Binds cyanide directly, forming cyanocobalamin |

## Two antidotes worth extra caution

- **Flumazenil**: reverses benzodiazepine sedation, but in a patient who
  is physically dependent it can precipitate withdrawal seizures — reserve
  for carefully selected cases (e.g., isolated benzodiazepine overdose in
  a naive patient), not routine reversal.
- **Naloxone**: has a shorter half-life than most opioids it's reversing,
  so a patient can re-sedate after an initial response — plan for
  repeat dosing or an infusion, not a single dose and discharge.

## Toxidrome pattern recognition

- **Anticholinergic**: "hot as a hare, blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red
  as a beet, mad as a hatter" — hyperthermia, mydriasis, dry skin,
  flushing, delirium.
- **Cholinergic (organophosphates)**: SLUDGE — salivation, lacrimation,
  urination, diarrhea, GI upset, emesis — plus miosis and bradycardia.
- **Sympathomimetic** (cocaine, amphetamines): hypertension, tachycardia,
  hyperthermia, diaphoresis, mydriasis, agitation.
- **Opioid**: the classic triad — miosis, respiratory depression,
  decreased level of consciousness.

## Key teaching point

When a vignette gives you a toxidrome instead of a named substance, name
the toxidrome first, out loud, before reaching for a specific antidote —
it's the fastest way to avoid anchoring on the wrong toxin.
