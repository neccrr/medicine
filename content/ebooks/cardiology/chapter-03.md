# Chapter 3: Arrhythmias at the Bedside

Arrhythmia interpretation is less about memorizing every possible rhythm and
more about a repeatable systematic approach: rate, rhythm, axis, then
pattern-match.

## A systematic ECG approach

1. **Rate** — fast (>100), slow (<60), or normal?
2. **Regularity** — regular, regularly irregular, or irregularly irregular?
3. **P waves** — present before every QRS? Normal morphology?
4. **QRS width** — narrow (<120ms, supraventricular origin) or wide
   (ventricular origin or aberrant conduction)?
5. **Pattern-match** against the classic rhythms below.

## High-yield rhythms

- **Atrial fibrillation**: irregularly irregular, no discrete P waves,
  narrow QRS (unless aberrancy). Manage with rate/rhythm control and stroke
  risk stratification (CHA₂DS₂-VASc score).
- **Atrial flutter**: regular, sawtooth flutter waves, often 2:1 conduction
  (ventricular rate ~150).
- **SVT (AVNRT/AVRT)**: regular, narrow, fast (150–250), sudden onset/offset.
  First-line: vagal maneuvers, then adenosine.
- **Ventricular tachycardia**: regular, wide QRS, rate >100. Treat as
  unstable if hypotensive/altered — synchronized cardioversion. Stable
  monomorphic VT can be trialed on antiarrhythmics.
- **Torsades de pointes**: polymorphic VT with a twisting QRS axis,
  associated with QT prolongation. Treat with IV magnesium.
- **Third-degree (complete) heart block**: P waves and QRS complexes are
  completely dissociated, each marching to its own regular rate. Usually
  needs pacing.

## Special case: WPW with atrial fibrillation

Look for a short PR interval and delta wave at baseline. If a WPW patient
develops AFib, **avoid AV nodal blocking agents** (adenosine, beta-blockers,
calcium channel blockers, digoxin) — blocking the AV node can push
conduction entirely down the accessory pathway and precipitate ventricular
fibrillation. Use procainamide or synchronized cardioversion instead.

## Key teaching point

"Regularly irregular" (a repeating pattern of irregularity, like grouped
beats) points toward things like second-degree AV block (Mobitz I/II);
"irregularly irregular" (no pattern at all) is the hallmark of atrial
fibrillation.
