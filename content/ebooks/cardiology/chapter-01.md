# Chapter 1: Approach to Chest Pain

Chest pain is one of the most common — and highest-stakes — presenting
complaints in medicine. The goal of the initial evaluation is always the
same: rule out the immediately life-threatening causes before working
through the rest of the differential.

## The "can't-miss" diagnoses

1. **Acute coronary syndrome** — crushing, pressure-like pain, often with
   radiation to the arm/jaw, diaphoresis, dyspnea. Get an ECG within 10
   minutes of presentation.
2. **Aortic dissection** — tearing pain, often radiating to the back,
   asymmetric pulses or blood pressures between arms.
3. **Pulmonary embolism** — pleuritic pain, tachycardia, risk factors
   (immobility, malignancy, prior VTE).
4. **Tension pneumothorax** — sudden dyspnea, absent breath sounds,
   tracheal deviation, hemodynamic instability.
5. **Cardiac tamponade** — Beck's triad (hypotension, JVD, muffled heart
   sounds), often following pericarditis or trauma.
6. **Esophageal rupture (Boerhaave syndrome)** — severe pain after forceful
   vomiting, subcutaneous emphysema.

## History clues that shift probability

- **Pleuritic** pain (worse with breathing) → pericarditis, PE, pneumonia,
  pneumothorax rather than ACS.
- **Positional** pain (better sitting forward) → pericarditis.
- **Reproducible with palpation** → musculoskeletal, though this does not
  fully exclude cardiac causes in a patient with risk factors.
- **Exertional, relieved by rest** → classic stable angina pattern.

## First-line workup

- 12-lead ECG immediately
- Troponin (serial if initial is borderline/negative and suspicion remains)
- Chest X-ray
- Consider D-dimer / CT-PE protocol if PE is on the differential
- Bedside ultrasound for tamponade/pneumothorax when available

## Key teaching point

Normal vital signs do not rule out a life-threatening cause. Risk-stratify
using clinical gestalt plus validated scores (e.g., HEART score for ACS)
rather than reassurance from a single normal parameter.
